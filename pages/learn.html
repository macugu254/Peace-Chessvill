1The idea in one minute

Somewhere on a tiny board, a black king is hiding. Everyone at the table has a hand of cards. Each card is a white chess piece on a named square. Your job is to find a way to trap the king using your cards. The first player to prove a trap wins the game.

The goalBuild a checkmate with your cards, before anyone else does. Win 3 games to win the tournament.

Nobody needs a real board, because the position lives in your head. That is the point of the game: it trains you to picture a chess position without looking at one. If you are just starting, the game has a Show board switch that draws the board for you.

Words in bold are the ones you will meet again. The cheat sheet at the end lists them all.

2The board and its square names

The board is a 4 by 4 grid of 16 squares. It is one corner of a normal chessboard. The columns are called files and use letters: h, g, f, e. The rows are called ranks and use numbers: 8, 7, 6, 5.

A square's name is its file letter followed by its rank number. The top left corner is h8. Next to it is g8. Below h8 is h7.

You will see the board from the black king's side. That is why the letters run h, g, f, e from left to right and the 8 is at the top.

3Cards and pieces

Every card names a piece and a square, for example Qh6. Read it as Q (queen) + h6 (square h6). Pieces use these letters:

P
Pawn: sees the two squares diagonally in front of it, toward rank 8
N
Knight: jumps in an L shape
B
Bishop: slides along diagonals
R
Rook: slides along its file and rank
Q
Queen: slides like a rook and a bishop together
K
King: the black king you are hunting. King cards are black.

When a piece "sees" a square, we say it attacks it. If the black king stands on a square a piece attacks, the king is in check. Choose a piece and touch the board to see what it attacks. The yellow dots are the attacked squares.

With walls on, touch squares to build blockers. Pieces cannot see past them.

Sliding pieces (bishop, rook, queen) stop at the first card in their way. In the real game, your own placed cards can block each other, so watch for that.

4Check, escape, and checkmate

The black king never plays on its own. It only answers each card you place. These are its rules:

If it is in check
It must move to a safe square next to it, or eat an unprotected card next to it. If it has no way out, that is checkmate. You win.
If it is not in check
It stays where it is. It may only eat an unprotected card next to it.
Safe square
A square none of your cards attack.
Protected card
A card another of your cards also attacks. The king can never eat it.

Your cards are placed one at a time, in the order you choose. That order matters a lot. Watch three examples. Press Next to step through.

5What you do on your turn

Everyone holds 4 cards. The top card of the draw pile is always face up, so you know what you will draw next. On your turn you do one of these:

Discard and drawPut a card at the bottom of the deck and take the top card. This is how you improve your hand.
Cast a king cardPlay a black king card. The king moves to that card's square. You draw a card to replace it.
Announce a checkmateSay "checkmate in 2" (or 3, or 4). You keep that many cards face down and discard the rest.
PassDo nothing.

Announcing and defending

After you announce, every other player gets one turn to spoil your plan. Nobody can see your cards, so the best defence is to cast a king card and move the king. Your cards were picked for the old square and may not work on the new one. If several king cards are cast, the last one counts.

When your turn comes back, you reveal: you place your cards one at a time. If the last one is checkmate, you win. If not, you simply draw back up to 4 cards and play on. There is no penalty.

Swapping a pawn

A pawn on rank 8 (Ph8, Pg8, Pf8, Pe8) can be swapped, for free, for a stronger piece on the same square from the spare deck. You can swap as often as you like on your turn.

The clock

Each player has 15 minutes. The clock runs only on your own turn. If it reaches zero, you are out of that game.

6A whole game, slowly

Ann and Ben are playing. The game started with the black king on h8. Follow along.

  1. Ann's hand: Qh6 Qg7 Pe5 Bg5. She spots a mate right away: Qh6, then Qg7 (this is Example 1). She announces "checkmate in 2", keeps Qh6 and Qg7 face down, and discards Pe5 and Bg5.
  2. Ben's hand: Kf6 Qh5 Pe6 Rg5. He cannot see Ann's cards, but he knows something is coming. He casts the king card Kf6. The king moves from h8 to f6, in the middle, where it has the most room. He draws the face-up top card: Qf7.
  3. Ann reveals. Her turn is back. Watch what happens to her plan against a king on f6:
  1. Ann's mate failed, so she draws back up to 4 cards (she gets two new cards, no king card among them). Her turn ends.
  2. Ben's next turn. His hand is now Qh5 Pe6 Rg5 Qf7. With the king on f6, Qh5 and Qf7 make a mate. He announces "checkmate in 2" and discards Pe6 and Rg5.
  3. Ann's chance to defend. She holds no king card and no pawn on rank 8 to swap, so she cannot move the king. She does something else.
  4. Ben reveals. Qh5 first, then Qf7:

What Ben did right: he moved the king into the middle when he was attacked, kept a second plan in his hand, and chose a mate that fitted the king's new square.

7Practice: find the mate

Each puzzle gives you the king's square and four cards. Pick two cards in the order you would place them, then press Reveal. The game's own rules check your answer.

8Common mistakes and questions

Mistakes everyone makes at first

Lonely card
Placing a card next to the king with nothing protecting it. The king eats it.
Wrong order
The same two cards can win in one order and fail in the other.
Pawn direction
Pawns attack the squares diagonally ahead, not straight ahead.
Forgetting defenders
Your mate fits the king's square today. A defender can change that square before you reveal.
Occupied squares
You cannot place a card on the king's square or on a square that already has a card.
King cards in a mate
King cards cannot be part of an announced mate. You are hunting the king, not using it.

Questions

Do I need to know chess?No. You need the six piece names and how they see squares, which chapter 3 covers.
Is it luck or skill?Both. You get your cards by luck, and you win by spotting what they can do, choosing the right order, and defending well.
How do I remember where the king is?The king's square is public and cast king cards stay on the table. At the start, keep the Show board switch on.
Can I see the other players' cards?No. You only see your own hand, how many cards each player holds, the face-up top card, and every swap.
What if my announced mate does not work?Nothing bad happens beyond losing your turn. You draw back up to 4 cards.
What is the spare deck?A small deck of the 20 non-pawn cards on rank 8. It is where a swapped pawn gets its new piece.
How do I win the whole tournament?Win 3 games before anyone else.
